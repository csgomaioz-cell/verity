The field log: what it did with nobody at the desk

Not a demo. The actual record of one assistant, on one Windows PC, working a real job unattended — including the parts that did not go well.

Everybody selling an AI assistant shows you a clip of it answering a question. That proves nothing about the hours when you are not watching. So instead, here is the job I actually gave VERITY and what it did, hour by hour, on 22 November 2026 — with the misses left in.

The job it was given

One standing order, written once, running on a schedule on my own PC several times a day: find small firms who might want this installed, write to each one personally, answer anyone who replies, keep a status file, and do not break these rules. No one is at the desk when it runs. There is nobody to ask. It re-reads its own status file at the start of every run, so each run continues the last one instead of starting over.

What happened that day

Morning Research, then fourteen letters

It briefed background research workers to find small professional firms with a published contact address, read what came back, and wrote a separate email for each one in Spanish — each naming something specific and checkable on that firm's own site, and each ending with a line saying that a one-word "no" means they are never contacted again. Fourteen were delivered from my ordinary Gmail. No template blast: fourteen different letters.

13:18 A reply arrives, and it is a no

One recipient — a marketing consultant — answered in two and a half hours with a rate card: she only works on a paid basis, $300 for a product placement. VERITY handled the whole thread itself. It declined the spend, because buying coverage before a single customer exists is the wrong order to do things in, and counter-offered a free install plus three months of maintenance in exchange for an honest review she was free to make negative. She declined that too. It closed the thread politely at 15:28, logged it as a no, and marked her never to be contacted again. Four messages, no sale, nobody at the desk for any of it.

Afternoon It built this website

The page you are on, the home page and the safety page were written and deployed by VERITY, including the commits. That is also the honest caveat about this site: the product wrote its own shopfront.

Evening It stopped, on purpose

The order says at most fifteen emails a day. Fifteen were spent. Two later runs found a queue of written, ready-to-send letters sitting there and sent none of them, because the cap was reached. It swept the inbox for replies instead, found none, wrote the next day's batch, and filed its log.

What went wrong

  • One address was dead. A prospect's domain did not resolve; the email bounced. It logged the bounce, did not retry, and did not go hunting for a personal address for that person.
  • It sent a wrong link and corrected itself. One email carried an out-of-date address for this site. It noticed, sent a one-line correction naming the mistake, and said sorry for the second email. I would rather that than a dead link nobody mentions.
  • A research worker failed outright on one task and returned nothing. The run carried on with what it had.
  • No sale yet. One reply out of fourteen, and that reply was a no. This page is a record, not a victory lap.

What it would not decide alone

The same order lists the things it must stop and ask about, by phone, rather than handle: spending any money, entering a password, creating an account, registering a business, and a customer who is ready to pay. In a full day of working alone it did not cross one of them. It also never pasted, emailed or published a key, a token or a session file — that is a standing rule, checked at the end of every single run.

It phones my mobile for those, says why it is calling, and waits. A day with no call means nothing happened that was mine to decide.

Why this is the proof worth showing

The interesting claim about an assistant like this is not that it can write an email. It is that it can be given a job with rules, left alone for a day, and found afterwards to have done the job, kept the rules, recorded what it did, stopped when a limit said stop, and handled a stranger's reply well enough that the stranger answered twice more. That is the thing being sold, and the log above is the whole of it.

What this is not

It is not an independent test. I wrote the order, I own the PC, and I am the one telling you what happened; the first customer install will produce a case study written with the customer instead, and that will be worth more than this page. There is no recorded demo on this page yet either — reply to me and I will walk you through it live on a screen-share instead of asking you to trust a video.
